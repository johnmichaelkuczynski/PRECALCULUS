# 🔢 Precalculus — A Four-Week Course

### Build confidence with functions, algebra, and trigonometry—one clear idea at a time.

🟦 **Four weeks** &nbsp; 🟪 **29 concise lectures** &nbsp; 🟩 **Practice, homework, and exams**

Precalculus connects algebraic tools to the functions and patterns that prepare you for calculus. Learn from short, focused lessons, then put each idea to work with problems, feedback, and help from an AI tutor.

> **Start anywhere, study at your own pace, and use the built-in math keyboard whenever symbols get in the way.**

---

## 🌈 A four-week path through precalculus

| Week | Theme | Explore |
|:---:|---|---|
| 🟦 **1** | **Functions and their graphs** | Real numbers and intervals; function notation, graphs and transformations; combining and composing functions; inverses; linear functions and rates of change |
| 🟪 **2** | **Polynomial and rational functions** | Quadratics and polynomial graphs; polynomial division and zeros; complex numbers; rational functions, asymptotes, and inequalities |
| 🟩 **3** | **Exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions** | Exponential and logarithmic rules, growth and decay; angles and radians; the unit circle; sine and cosine graphs |
| 🟧 **4** | **Trigonometry and further connections** | Identities, inverse trigonometric functions, and the laws of sines and cosines; polar coordinates, complex numbers, conics, vectors, sequences, series, and a capstone synthesis |

## ✨ Learn, try, and make sense of it

- **Focused lessons with room to go deeper.** Follow the short lecture or choose a longer explanation when you want more context.
- **Write mathematics naturally.** Use the on-screen math keyboard in tutor conversations, practice, and assignment or exam answers. Enter familiar notation without needing to remember every symbol.
- **Practise at a level that responds to you.** Topic practice uses your prior performance to choose a starting challenge and adjusts difficulty as you work.
- **Get help in context.** Ask the AI tutor about a lesson or practice problem, request a hint or explanation, and keep the discussion connected to what you are studying.
- **Learn from feedback.** Review explanations for your answers, then ask follow-up questions when a step or idea is still unclear.
- **Build toward assessments.** Work through practice and homework, then review tests, a midterm, and a final.
- **See what to revisit.** Check your activity and topic-level progress to decide where another round of practice could help.
