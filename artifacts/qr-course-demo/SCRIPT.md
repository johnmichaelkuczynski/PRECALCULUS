# Economics 101 — 40-second screen-led promotion

## Director's treatment

- Promise: Show that Economics 101 takes students from a clear course plan to explanations, guided practice, feedback, and visible progress.
- Identity: Keep the existing Economics 101 interface, cream-and-navy palette, serif headings, and simple iconography.
- Hero: The app itself. The course UI fills the complete 16:9 frame in every shot; there is no device mockup, title card, or marketing overlay.
- Motion: A small cursor performs visible clicks; the app changes screens; progress figures and tutor answers reveal in place.
- Arc: Start with the course overview, move through learning and practice, then end on reviewed work.
- Payoff: The final feedback screen resolves the course journey inside the app rather than switching to an external end card.
- Sound: One friendly, authoritative British male voiceover; no music, sound effects, or other audio.

## Shot-by-shot script

### Shot 01 / 00.00–06.50 / 6.50 seconds — Course at a glance

Purpose: Establish that the course is organized and immediately usable.

Composition: Full-bleed 16:9 app interface, matching the existing course design. The left navigation, course heading, recent activity, and weekly schedule remain visible as genuine interface elements. No external text, panels, or end-card graphics cover the app.

Exact screen copy: Sidebar “Dashboard,” “Assignments,” and “Analytics”; dashboard heading “Economics”; “Recent Activity”; “Course Schedule”; Week 1 card “Week 1 — Foundations of choice and markets”; “4 Lectures”; “2 Assignments.” After the click, the Week 1 page shows “Week 1 — Foundations of choice and markets” and the lecture list.

Voiceover: “Your dashboard keeps the four-week course, assignments, and recent progress in one clear view.”

Choreography: 00.00–00.80 the full app view settles with the cursor already visible. 00.80–02.35 the cursor moves to the Week 1 card. 02.35–02.70 it clicks; the week view resolves in place. 02.70–04.25 the cursor moves to the first lecture. 04.25–04.60 it clicks. 04.60–06.50 the lecture layout arrives and remains readable.

Transition: The selected Week 1 lecture opens directly into Shot 02; the app canvas and navigation stay continuous.

### Shot 02 / 06.50–13.00 / 6.50 seconds — Learn at the right depth

Purpose: Show that students can change reading depth and move naturally into practice.

Composition: Keep the course's two-column lecture layout full-screen. The economics reading occupies the left half; lesson tools occupy the right half. The course content stays unobscured.

Exact screen copy: “1.1 What economics is and why it matters”; “Short,” “Medium,” “Long”; “What is economics?”; “Ask the tutor”; “Practice on this”; “Generated Practice Problem.” The reading explains scarcity and opportunity cost. The practice prompt asks students to set quantity demanded equal to quantity supplied and solve for the market-clearing price.

Voiceover: “Open a lesson, choose a short or long explanation, and follow the ideas at your own pace.”

Choreography: 00.00–00.70 the two-column lecture view settles. 00.70–02.15 the cursor selects “Long”; the reading expands. 02.15–03.65 it selects “Practice on this”; the practice prompt appears. 03.65–05.70 it returns to “Ask the tutor.” 05.70–06.50 the cursor leaves the question field ready for the next shot.

Transition: The open lesson and its market-equilibrium question carry into the tutor conversation.

### Shot 03 / 13.00–19.50 / 6.50 seconds — Ask the tutor

Purpose: Demonstrate question-led help that stays anchored to the course reading.

Composition: Preserve the same full-screen lesson layout: reading on the left, conversation on the right. The student question and answer appear inside the app's normal chat area, not as floating promotional copy.

Exact screen copy: Lesson heading “1.1 What economics is and why it matters”; tab “Ask the tutor”; question “Why does a price above equilibrium create a surplus?”; tutor answer “When a price is above equilibrium, sellers want to supply more than buyers want to purchase. The unsold inventory is a surplus, which puts downward pressure on price. Markets tend to move toward the point where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.”

Voiceover: “Ask the tutor about a lesson and get a focused explanation with practical examples.”

Choreography: 00.00–00.90 the cursor enters the question box. 00.90–02.10 the question types in. 02.10–02.45 the cursor clicks send. 02.45–03.15 the reply indicator appears. 03.15–05.80 the tutor explanation streams into the chat. 05.80–06.50 the answer remains readable while the cursor moves to Analytics.

Transition: The cursor's navigation click reveals the course's progress view.

### Shot 05 / 26.00–33.50 / 7.50 seconds — Practice with feedback

Purpose: Show students applying a core economics model and receiving a useful correction.

Composition: Keep the practice assignment in the app's full-screen content area. The question, answer field, on-screen Economics math keys, and feedback remain visible in the normal interface.

Exact screen copy: “Supply and demand”; “A market has demand Qd = 100 - 2P and supply Qs = 20 + 2P. What price clears the market?”; answer “P = 20”; feedback “Correct!” and “Equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied: Qd = Qs. This market-clearing condition is the starting point for analyzing prices, shortages, and surpluses.”

Voiceover: “Guided practice turns supply and demand into a real problem, with feedback after each attempt.”

Choreography: 00.00–00.75 the problem appears. 00.75–02.70 the cursor selects the answer field and enters “P = 20.” 02.70–03.00 it clicks Submit. 03.00–03.80 a brief incorrect-attempt explanation appears only as part of the practice flow. 03.80–05.25 the correct answer is entered. 05.25–05.55 the cursor submits again. 05.55–07.50 the correct feedback remains readable as the cursor moves to Assignments.

Transition: The Assignments navigation click opens the reviewed course work.

### Shot 06 / 33.50–40.00 / 6.50 seconds — Review and continue

Purpose: End on useful feedback and the next step in the course, not an external sales card.

Composition: Full-screen assignment review in the original app layout. The submitted work, result, and tutor feedback are the only focus. Do not cover the interface with a title or call to action.

Exact screen copy: “Assignments”; “Homework 1.1 — Scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost”; “Review: Homework 1.1”; the opportunity-cost question; answer “Opportunity cost = $40”; “AI Tutor Feedback”; “Correct. The $40 in forgone wages is the opportunity cost of studying. Good economic reasoning compares the next-best alternative, not just the direct price.”

Voiceover: “Review your assignment results, understand the reasoning, and keep building confidence across the whole course.”

Choreography: 00.00–00.70 the cursor selects Assignments. 00.70–01.70 the Week 1 work list settles. 01.70–02.15 it opens the first homework review. 02.15–03.20 the submitted answer and result resolve. 03.20–05.30 tutor feedback appears and stays readable. 05.30–06.50 the cursor exits the content area; the full app screen holds through the loop handoff.

Transition: Hold the course review to the final frame, then cut back to the opening dashboard on the loop.
